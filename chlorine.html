Cl

Basic Information

Atomic Number : 17
Atomic Mass : 35.45
Proton Number : 17
Neutron Number : 17
Category : Post-transition Metal

History of Chlorine

Chlorine was first discovered in 1774 by Swedish chemist Carl Wilhelm Scheele. He produced chlorine gas while reacting hydrochloric acid with manganese dioxide. In 1810, English chemist Humphry Davy proved that chlorine was an element and gave it its name. The name chlorine comes from the Greek word "chloros," meaning pale green, because of its colour. Today, chlorine is widely used in water treatment, chemicals, and many industrial processes.

Physical Properties ⌄

State at room temperature: Gas Colour: Yellowish-green Odour: Strong, sharp, choking smell Taste: Not applicable; toxic gas Density: 3.21 g/L Melting point: −101.5°C Boiling point: −34.04°C Solubility: Slightly soluble in water Molecular form: Usually exists as Cl₂ molecules

Chemical Properties ⌄

Chlorine is a highly reactive non-metal and belongs to the halogen group. It reacts with hydrogen to form hydrogen chloride gas: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl Chlorine reacts with metals to form metal chlorides. It can react with water to form hydrochloric acid and hypochlorous acid: Cl₂ + H₂O → HCl + HClO Chlorine is a strong oxidizing agent and can kill bacteria by destroying microorganisms.

Occurrence ⌄

Chlorine is not usually found as a free element in nature because it is highly reactive. It is mainly found in compounds called chlorides. The largest natural source of chlorine is sodium chloride (NaCl), which is found in seawater and rock salt. Chlorine compounds are also found in living organisms and minerals.

Uses ⌄

Chlorine is commonly used to disinfect drinking water and swimming pools. It is used to make bleach and cleaning products. Chlorine compounds are used to produce PVC plastic, which is used in pipes and construction materials. It is also used in making medicines, solvents, and many industrial chemicals. Chlorine helps prevent the spread of harmful bacteria and viruses.

Interesting facts ⌄

Chlorine is a yellowish-green gas with a strong smell. Chlorine is essential for safe drinking water because it kills harmful microorganisms. The name chlorine comes from the Greek word "chloros," meaning pale green. Chlorine gas was used as a chemical weapon during World War I. Chlorine is the second element in the halogen group after fluorine. Table salt (NaCl) contains chlorine combined with sodium.
