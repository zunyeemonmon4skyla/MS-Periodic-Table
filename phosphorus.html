P

Basic Information

Atomic Number : 15
Atomic Mass : 30.97
Proton Number : 15
Neutron Number : 15
Category : Non - Metal

History of Phosphorus

Phosphorus was discovered in 1669 by German scientist Hennig Brand. He discovered phosphorus while experimenting with urine and accidentally produced a glowing substance. The name phosphorus comes from the Greek word meaning "light-bringer" because white phosphorus glows in the dark. Later, phosphorus became an important element used in fertilizers, chemicals, and industry.

Physical Properties ⌄

State at room temperature: Solid Colour:White, red, or black depending on form Odour: White phosphorus has a garlic-like odour Taste: Not recommended to taste; toxic Density: 1.82 g/cm³ (white phosphorus) Melting point: 44.1°C (white phosphorus) Boiling point: 280.5°C Solubility: Insoluble in water Molecular form: Usually exists as P₄ molecules

Chemical Properties ⌄

Phosphorus is a reactive non-metal. White phosphorus reacts easily with oxygen and can ignite in air: P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀ It can react with metals to form metal phosphides. Phosphorus forms compounds with hydrogen, oxygen, and halogens. Red phosphorus is less reactive and more stable than white phosphorus. Phosphorus can form different allotropes, including white, red, and black phosphorus.

Occurrence ⌄

Phosphorus is not found as a free element in nature because it is highly reactive. It is mainly found in phosphate minerals such as apatite. Phosphorus is present in living organisms, including bones, teeth, DNA, and cells. Large deposits of phosphate rocks are used to produce phosphorus compounds and fertilizers.

Uses ⌄

Phosphorus is mainly used to make fertilizers that help plants grow. It is used in the production of detergents, pesticides, and chemicals. Red phosphorus is used on the striking surface of safety matches. Phosphorus compounds are used in making fireworks and some types of flame retardants. Phosphorus is also important in biology because it is part of DNA and energy molecules in living organisms.

Interesting facts ⌄

Phosphorus was the first element discovered by humans. The name phosphorus means "light-bringer" because white phosphorus glows in the dark. White phosphorus can ignite when exposed to air. Phosphorus is essential for life and is found in DNA and bones. There are several forms of phosphorus called allotropes, including white, red, and black phosphorus. Most phosphorus on Earth is found in phosphate rocks rather than as pure phosphorus.
