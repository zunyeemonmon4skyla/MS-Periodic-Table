Basic Information

Atomic Number : 20
Atomic Mass : 40.08
Proton Number : 20
Neutron Number : 20
Category : Alkaline Earth Metal

History of Calcium

Calcium was discovered in 1808 by British chemist Humphry Davy. He isolated calcium by using electrolysis on a mixture of calcium oxide and mercury oxide. The name calcium comes from the Latin word "calx," meaning lime. Calcium compounds have been used since ancient times in building materials such as limestone and cement. Today, calcium is known as an important element for living organisms and industry.

Physical Properties ⌄

State at room temperature: Solid Colour: Silvery-grey Odour: Odourless Taste: Not applicable; reactive metal Density: 1.55 g/cm³ Melting point: 842°C Boiling point: 1484°C Solubility: Reacts slowly with water Molecular form: Usually exists as calcium atoms (Ca)

Chemical Properties ⌄

Calcium is a reactive alkaline earth metal. It reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide and hydrogen gas: Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂ Calcium reacts with oxygen to form calcium oxide: 2Ca + O₂ → 2CaO It reacts with acids to produce calcium salts and hydrogen gas. Calcium forms ionic compounds with non-metals such as chlorine to produce calcium chloride (CaCl₂).

Occurrence ⌄

Calcium is the fifth most abundant element in the Earth's crust. It is not found as a pure element because it reacts easily with other substances. Calcium is mainly found in minerals such as limestone, gypsum, and calcium carbonate. It is also found in living organisms, especially in bones, teeth, and shells.

Uses ⌄

Calcium is used in the production of cement, concrete, and building materials. Calcium compounds are used in making glass and treating acidic soil. Calcium is essential for living organisms because it helps build strong bones and teeth. Calcium is used in medicines and dietary supplements. Calcium compounds are also used in industries such as metal production and agriculture.

Interesting facts ⌄

Calcium is the fifth most abundant element in the Earth's crust. Calcium is the most abundant metal in the human body. About 99% of the body's calcium is stored in bones and teeth. Calcium compounds have been used for thousands of years in buildings and sculptures. Calcium burns with a brick-red flame when heated.
